I am Jiaxuan Li 李嘉轩 (pronounced as Lǐ Jiā Xuān ), a PhD candidate at Department of Astrophysical Sciences, Princeton University.

I'm an astrophysicist interested in galaxy formation and evolution, low-surface-brightness astrophysics, sky surveys, machine learning, and instrumentation. My current research focuses on uncovering the formation and evolution of dwarf galaxies through observations and numerical simulations. I lead the ELVES-Dwarf survey, which probes the satellite populations of dwarf galaxies in the Local Volume. I work with data from cutting-edge deep-sky surveys such as the Hyper Suprime-Camera (HSC) Subaru Strategic Program (SSP) and the Merian Survey. I am also interested in applying advanced machine-learning techniques to astrophysical challenges. For more information, please visit Research.

Recently, together with Sihao Cheng (IAS) and Eritas Yang (Princeton), we discovered an exceptional trans-Neptunian object (TNO), 2017 OF201. Most strikingly, its orbit does not follow the apsidal-clustering pattern observed among many other extreme TNOs. This pattern has been interpreted as evidence for the gravitational influence of a massive planet beyond Neptune, often called Planet Nine or Planet X. The orbit of 2017 OF201 may therefore challenge that hypothesis. See our paper for details. This work has been featured by IAS and Princeton University, as well as in The New York Times, Sky & Telescope, Phys.org, New Scientist, Universe Today, and other outlets.

I am originally from Dingxi, Gansu Province, a small city in northwestern China. I became an amateur astronomer in sixth grade, and my enthusiasm led me to participate in the Chinese National Astronomy Olympiad and several international competitions. Before coming to Princeton, I completed my undergraduate studies in astrophysics at Peking University. Outside academia, I enjoy photography, hiking, climbing (but not bouldering!), and exploring whisky.
